SHIDOU KIRISAKI

"If I'm not alive, then I can't protect their lives."

BASIC INFORMATION

Shidou is a gentle, calm and composed prisoner who treats others as if they were his younger children. Despite being imprisoned with no knowledge about Milgram, Shidou has already come terms with his imprisonment and is also hoping for a death penalty.

PROFILE & ANALYSIS

Shidou Kirisaki was a physician working at a renowned university hospital, with a bright future ahead of him. At the age of 24, he married his childhood sweetheart of ten years, and was blessed with two children.

He was leading a picture-perfect life that anyone would aspire to.

He worked in the transplant department of the hospital, and specialized in organ transplants. He would often interact with the families of brain-dead patients, and was in a position to ask permission for their loved one’s organs. The families’ devastation, and his role in clinically ending the lives of these brain-dead patients did cause him heart-ache, but he also firmly believed that his work was essential to save the lives of the many patients awaiting organ transplants.

One evening, there was a collision between a taxi and a large commercial truck.

The victims who were brought into the hospital were his wife and two children. They had been heading to the hospital via taxi to bring a change of clothes for Shidou that he had forgotten that day. It was a devastating accident, and it was clear that both his wife and children would not survive without immediate care. It is generally forbidden for surgeons to perform on their family members.

Shidou was put in charge of the surgery for the driver, the very man who had just bulldozed his entire family.

Shidou’s head was in a panic, but his hands moved automatically. He quietly killed the truck driver, taking care to make it look unintentional. He thought, the sooner he was done with this surgery, the sooner he could take care of his own family.

With the situation being an emergency, Shidou was given an exception, and was allowed to treat his family. Despite this, his wife and younger son died soon after.

In his despair, Shidou worked and prayed ferociously in hopes of at least saving his elder son.

However, after weeks of lingering in limbo between life and death, his elder son’s condition became all too clear to Shidou; the familiar condition of brain death.

Shidou could not bring himself to admit this was indeed death.

Shidou regretted and felt deeply ashamed of the role he had played in enticing the families of brain-dead patients to succumb and donate their loved one’s bodies for the good of some stranger. It is not ethical, it is inconsistent, it is ugly and evil. In a state of severe self loathing, Shidou rummages through his son’s belongings that were brought in from the scene of the accident. Disoriented and in a haze, he finds a piece of paper and tears it to shreds. It was the organ donor card that Shidou had repeatedly told his family to carry with them at all times.
